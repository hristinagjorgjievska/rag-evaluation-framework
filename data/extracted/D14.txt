What is machine learning?
•
A branch of artificial intelligence concerned with designing 
and developing algorithms that allow computers to evolve 
behaviors based on empirical data.
•
As intelligence requires knowledge, it is necessary for 
computers to acquire knowledge
•
Automating automation
•
Getting computers to program themselves
– Let the data do the work instead!
6


---------- страница 7 ----------
Machine Learning Definition 
•
The Formal one:
– “A computer program is said to learn from experience E 
concerning some class of tasks T and performance 
measure P if its performance at tasks in T, as measured 
by P, improves with experience E.”
•
A Practical Example:
– Look at data. Try something. Get the correct answer? No? 
Look at the data. Do something different. Better? Yes? Do 
that again. (Repeat)
•
Human learning is still far more sophisticated 
than even the most advanced machine-learning 
algorithms, but computers have the advantage 
of greater capacity to memorize, recall, and 
process data.

---------- страница 8 ----------
Types of Learning
•
Supervised (inductive) learning
– Labels provided
– Training data includes desired outputs
– Predicting the future. 
– Learn from known past examples to predict future
•
Unsupervised learning
– Labels not provided
– Training data does not include desired outputs
– Making sense of data
– Understanding the past
– Learning the structure of data
•
Semi-supervised learning
– Training data includes a few desired outputs
•
Reinforcement learning
– Rewards from sequence of actions

True vs. Statistical Model
•
We will assume that the response variable, 𝑌𝑌, relates to the 
predictors, 𝑋𝑋, through some unknown function expressed generally 
as:
𝑌𝑌= 𝑓𝑓𝑋𝑋+ 𝜀𝜀
•
Here, 𝑓𝑓 is the unknown function expressing an underlying rule for 
relating 𝑌𝑌 to 𝑋𝑋, 𝜀𝜀 is the random amount (unrelated to 𝑋𝑋) that 𝑌𝑌 differs 
from the rule 𝑓𝑓𝑋𝑋.
•
A statistical model is any algorithm that estimates 𝑓𝑓. 
We denote the estimated function as ෡𝑓𝑓.

Prediction vs. Estimation
•
For some problems, what's important is obtaining ̂𝑓𝑓, our estimate of 𝑓𝑓. 
These are called inference problems. 
•
When we use a set of measurements, (𝑥𝑥𝑖𝑖,1, … , 𝑥𝑥𝑖𝑖,𝑝𝑝) to predict a value for the 
response variable, we denote the predicted value by:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 ො𝑦𝑦𝑖𝑖= ̂𝑓𝑓(𝑥𝑥𝑖𝑖,1, … , 𝑥𝑥𝑖𝑖,𝑝𝑝).
•
For some problems, we don't care about the specific form of ̂𝑓𝑓, we just 
want to make our predictions ො𝑦𝑦’s as close to the observed values 𝑦𝑦’ as 
possible. These are called prediction problems.

Hypothesis testing is a formal process through which we evaluate the 
validity of a statistical hypothesis by considering evidence for or against 
the hypothesis gathered by random sampling of the data. 
1.
State the hypotheses, typically a null hypothesis, 𝐻𝐻0 and an

2. Choose a type of analysis, i.e. how to use sample data to evaluate the

4. Use the value of the test statistic to either reject or not reject the null 
hypothesis.

Error Types
•
A type I error (or error of the first kind) is the incorrect rejection of a true null hypothesis. 
–
Usually a type I error leads one to conclude that a supposed effect or relationship exists when in fact 
it doesn't. 
–
Examples of type I errors include a test that shows a patient to have a disease when in fact the 
patient does not have the disease, a fire alarm going on indicating a fire when in fact there is no fire, 
or an experiment indicating that a medical treatment should cure a disease when in fact it does not.
•
A type II error (or error of the second kind) is the failure to reject a false null hypothesis. 
–
Examples of type II errors would be a blood test failing to detect the disease it was designed to detect, 
in a patient who really has the disease; a fire breaking out and the fire alarm does not ring; or a 
clinical trial of a medical treatment failing to show that the treatment works when really it does
